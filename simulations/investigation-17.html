Investigation 17 — Crossing a Tipping Point

A positive feedback can amplify change without producing a tipping point. In this simplified model, test what happens when warming remains below—or crosses—an ice-loss threshold.

START — COLD EQUILIBRIUM WITH EXTENSIVE REFLECTIVE ICE
Start: The system is in a cold dynamic equilibrium. Extensive snow and ice reflect a large fraction of incoming sunlight, helping maintain relatively low stored energy.
Below the threshold: The smaller forcing warms the system and reduces some ice, but substantial reflective ice remains. The system changes without tipping into a different state.
At the threshold: The system has reached the modeled ice-loss threshold. Predict what will happen if warming continues.
After the tipping point: Rapid ice loss lowers albedo. More solar energy is absorbed, producing additional warming and still more ice loss. The system shifts toward a much warmer equilibrium.
Does reversing the forcing reverse the response? Removing the most recent greenhouse-gas increase cools the system somewhat, but the lost reflective ice does not immediately return. The system remains in a warmer, lower-albedo state.
Model limitation: This animation represents a general threshold response. Real ice sheets, sea ice, and snow cover respond at different rates and may have different thresholds.
